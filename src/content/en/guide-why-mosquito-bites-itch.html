A mosquito bite itches because your body reacts to the saliva the mosquito injects while she drinks. According to the CDC, a puffy, reddish bump usually appears a few minutes after the bite, and a hard, itchy, reddish-brown bump can follow a day or so later. Only female mosquitoes bite: most of them cannot produce eggs without a blood meal.

Which mosquitoes bite, and why?

Only females. Female mosquitoes bite people and animals to get a blood meal, and most cannot produce eggs without one. Males do not bite people or animals (CDC). Some species never bite at all: the adults of Toxorhynchites, the elephant mosquito, feed only on sugars (University of Florida).

What is in mosquito saliva?

Saliva is the mosquito's toolkit for drinking without being noticed. Skin and blood are built to stop bleeding, and the saliva works against that. A 2025 review estimates that mosquito saliva contains about 100 to 200 proteins, and that 30–40% of them do not resemble any known protein. One example from Aedes aegypti is aegyptin, which binds collagen and stops platelets from sticking together, so the blood keeps flowing. Other salivary compounds widen blood vessels (Guo et al., Biomolecules, 2025; Ribeiro et al., 2010).

Why does the itch start after she has gone?

The itch is your immune response, not the bite itself. The proteins in the saliva are foreign, and the body reacts to them; the CDC describes the result as a bump and itching. That reaction takes minutes to build, which is why the bump usually appears after the mosquito has left, and a second, harder bump can follow about a day later (CDC).

What does a bite look like over time?

WhenWhat you usually see (CDC)
During the biteOften nothing: the mosquito injects saliva while she drinks
A few minutes laterA puffy, reddish bump
About a day laterA hard, itchy, reddish-brown bump, or several
If it gets worseRedness, warmth or a red streak spreading from the bite can mean infection: see a healthcare provider

What helps a mosquito bite stop itching?

The CDC suggests:

  1. Apply an ice pack for 10 minutes to reduce swelling and itching.
  2. Try a paste of baking soda and water.
  3. Use an over-the-counter anti-itch or antihistamine cream.
  4. Do not scratch: bites can become infected.

See a healthcare provider if symptoms get worse. This page is general information, not medical advice. In many parts of the world mosquito bites can also transmit viruses and parasites; the CDC's prevention guide lists the repellents it recommends.

How does Mosquito Simulator model the itch?

[[img:bite-wrist|Close-up of a mosquito drinking from a sleeper's wrist with a red welt|In the game, the welt swells while you drink.]]

The itch is the clock of every bite in the game. While you drink, a ring fills around the BITE button:

  • Gold: just before the itch arrives, the ring turns gold. Let go now for a perfect bite and 25% more blood.
  • Red: hold too long and the sleeper feels the itch and reacts: a slap, a roll onto the bitten spot, or waking up.
  • Itch delay: species differ. Toxorhynchites bites go unnoticed 40% longer than Culex, Culiseta and Deinocerites 20% longer, Anopheles 15% longer; Aedes aegypti and the gallinipper are noticed 10% sooner. Compare them on the species page.

The game compresses minutes into seconds and makes the itch arrive while the mosquito is still there; real bites usually start to itch after she has flown. To see what draws mosquitoes in the first place, read how mosquitoes find you.
